How it works

How the games are drawn, how pistes are filled and how teams are ranked, format by format.

Every competition

  • Only teams that are checked in go into the draw. In Swiss and mêlée, late arrivals join the next round.
  • Pistes fill themselves as games finish. A team is never on two pistes at once.
  • The winner reaches the points to win (13 by default). There are no draws; "Ended on time" allows a lower winning score.
  • A bye counts as a win, 13–7 by default.
  • Standings are worked out from the results every time. The last tie-break is a drawn lot, fixed for each team.

Formats

Swiss

Everyone plays every round, against teams with the same record.

Draw
Round 1 is random. After that, teams on the same number of wins meet, closest in the table first, with no rematches.
Ties
Wins, then the order you choose in Setup. Default: points difference, Buchholz, fine Buchholz, points for.
Then
Afternoon knockouts.

Snake

Every round drawn in advance, with no repeat games.

Draw
Teams stand in two lines and one line moves along a place each round, so team 2 plays whoever team 1 just played.
Ties
Wins, points difference, Buchholz, fine Buchholz, points for.
Then
Afternoon knockouts.

Barrage

Small groups in the morning, knockouts by group place in the afternoon.

Draw
Groups of 4 (or the size you set). A group of 4 can play a French poule: winners play winners, losers play losers, then a play-off for 2nd. Other groups play everyone in the group.
Ties
Wins, points difference, points for, then the games between the tied teams.
Then
Knockouts by group place, such as 1st and 2nd into the A, 3rd and 4th into the B.

Leagues

A few leagues, each with its own table.

Draw
You choose how many leagues. Teams are dealt in at random and play everyone in their league.
Ties
Wins, fewest losses, points difference, points for, then the games between the tied teams.
Then
Knockouts by league place.

Round robin

Everyone plays everyone once.

Draw
The whole schedule is drawn at the start.
Ties
Wins, points difference, points for, then the games between the tied teams.
Then
Afternoon knockouts.

Knockout

Lose and you're out.

Draw
One bracket, drawn at random or seeded in entry order. Top seeds get byes when the numbers don't fit, and seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final.
Then
An optional consolante for teams that lose their first game, and an optional third-place game.

Inter-club

Clubs enter several teams and play club against club.

Draw
Every club plays every other club, spread over the days you set. In each fixture the teams meet like for like, in rotation, or everyone against everyone. A team never plays its own club.
Ties
Clubs: games won by their teams, points difference, points for.
Then
An optional day 2 for 8 clubs, seeded from the day 1 table.

Mêlée

Players enter on their own and are drawn into teams.

Draw
New teams every round, or all rounds drawn in advance, avoiding repeat partners and opponents. Or teams drawn once, then any format.
Ties
Players are ranked on their own: wins, points difference, points for.
Then
Teams drawn once can go on to afternoon knockouts.

Precision shooting

Tir de précision to FIPJP rules.

Draw
Everyone shoots a qualifying scorecard: 5 ateliers from 6 to 9 m, each shot worth 0, 1, 3 or 5, out of 100.
Ties
Count-back (most 5s, then most 3s), then a shoot-out. Only settled when it decides something.
Then
A top 8 or top 16 knockout of duels, or a final.

Afternoon knockouts

  • You choose which places go into which knockout, such as the top 8 into the Main and 9th to 16th into the Plate. A Silver knockout can take everyone else.
  • Teams are seeded from the table (1 v 8, 2 v 7), or drawn at random. Teams from the same group are kept apart in the first round.
  • Withdrawn teams are left out and the next team moves up. Each knockout can have a third-place game.
